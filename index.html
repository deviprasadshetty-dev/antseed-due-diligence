AntSeed is a real, working, actively developed peer-to-peer inference network — not vapour. We installed it, routed live requests, and got correct completions with a median of 666 ms. The blocker is not the software; it is everything around the software. There is no uptime commitment, no status page, no independent contract audit, no privacy attestation that is actually live, and an open, confirmed bug that over-bills buyers. Total network revenue to date is roughly $317,000 lifetime, and the single largest seller takes 16.7% of it.

Revisit when AntSeed: publishes a status page with public uptime figures · closes issue #1200 · ships TEE verification in a released rather than "future" version · publishes an independent smart-contract audit of AntseedDeposits · and completes six months of on-chain settlement without a material incident.
01

Executive summary

AntSeed lets strangers sell AI model access directly to each other, paid in USDC cryptocurrency, skipping the big cloud companies. It works — we tested it and got a correct answer in under a second. We do not recommend it for production inference yet.

Three reasons:

  1. Nobody is accountable if it goes down. There is no service-level agreement, no status page, and no uptime history. An independent reviewer scored its "liquidity and access" readiness 1 out of 15. When we sent 20 requests to the most-used model, all 20 went to a single computer — so in practice there is no backup.
  2. You cannot be sure which model you got. The same model name returned different actual models depending on which seller served it, and one reply described itself as a different version than the one requested. AntSeed's own documentation admits model verification is not part of its trust score.
  3. Money and compliance risk is unmanaged. A confirmed, still-open bug lets sellers charge buyers roughly $0.50 more than they should. The contracts holding customer money have never been audited. The token cannot be sold or transferred at all.

Recommended use: internal experiments, developer tooling and cost-sensitive batch work where a failure costs time rather than money or reputation. Revisit in six months.

03

What it is and how it works

One paragraph, plain English.

AntSeed is an open-source protocol that turns AI model access into a peer-to-peer marketplace. Instead of sending every request to OpenAI or Anthropic, software running on your own machine discovers independent computers advertising models, opens an encrypted direct connection to one of them, sends the prompt, and pays that seller a fraction of a cent per thousand tokens in USDC cryptocurrency settled on the Base blockchain. There is no AntSeed account and no AntSeed server in the request path — your own machine is the router. In exchange for removing the middleman, you accept that the individual seller decides its own uptime, sees your plaintext prompt, and could in principle serve you something other than the model it advertised.

Reliability and readiness findings

5.1  Maturity

Verified The main repository was created 24 February 2026 — roughly eight months old. The latest release is v0.2.65, published 18 October 2026, the same day as this review. The project is pre-1.0 and on a 0.2.x line. #41 #43 #44

Verified Release cadence is genuinely fast and healthy. The 50 most recent releases span 7 August to 18 October 2026, with multiple releases per day in the final two weeks. Commits in the most recent 100 came from four identities, dominated by two active maintainers. There are 26 contributors, 123 stars and 43 forks. 64 issues are open, and 83 of the 100 most recent repository items are pull requests. #43 #44 #46 #49

This is the strongest thing about AntSeed. Verified The commit log reads like real work, not activity theatre: fix(node): Recover channels that ended on-chain, fix(buyer): drop services a seller stopped advertising on peer refresh, fix(cli): Do not treat an interrupted stream as a served answer. Maintainers are visibly fixing correctness bugs they found themselves. The code is not the problem.
Against that: Verified 64 open issues and a 0.2.x version mean no stability commitment exists. Semver itself puts 0.x outside the compatibility guarantee. There is no published deprecation policy, no long-term-support branch, and no changelog outside GitHub releases. #46

5.2  Uptime, SLA and incident history

Verified There is no status page. status.antseed.com does not resolve (DNS NXDOMAIN). antseed.statuspage.io returns a generic Atlassian marketing page, meaning no such Statuspage exists. Neither appears in the site sitemap or anywhere in the documentation. #1 #36 #37

No evidence found No SLA, no uptime percentage, no error-budget policy, no credits for downtime, no support channel, no on-call commitment and no published incident history. The Terms explicitly disclaim all warranties and state the protocol is provided "AS IS" and "WITH ALL FAULTS." #34

Inference AntSeed could not offer a meaningful SLA even if it wanted one, because the seller is an independent third party the Foundation explicitly does not control. That is architecturally honest and operationally fatal for anyone who needs a guarantee. If uptime matters to you, an SLA here would be a promise AntSeed could not keep.

5.3  Performance — what we measured, and what we could not

Verified Our own measurements on a free-tier model: median 666 ms, p95 2,078 ms end-to-end wall clock, with a 1.4-second warm path. That is competitive with a direct API call and is the single most encouraging result in this report. Because the buyer runs on your own machine and connects straight to the seller, there is no extra central hop.

Inference That speed came from a Google Cloud-hosted proxy at 34.9.72.40 that was itself calling DeepSeek's API. The latency we measured is therefore not the latency of a distributed compute network — it is the latency of a lightly-wrapped reseller. Do not extrapolate this number to workloads that need genuinely independent compute.

No evidence found No published benchmarks, no throughput figures, no rate-limit policy, no capacity guarantees and no independent latency study. The "97% below official API prices" headline repeated across launch coverage is AntSeed's own figure with no published breakdown; one trade publication independently flagged it as "a top-of-range claim rather than a steady-state price". #54

No evidence found We could not measure paid-model latency, cost or quality, because that required funding a wallet, which we did not do.

5.5  Provider quality control — failover, retries, bad actors

Claimed #8 Automatic routing "fails over to the next eligible provider after a retryable failure"; providers that recently failed are tried last; the router maintains failure streaks and cooldowns; and a request naming an unavailable model is refused (502) rather than silently downgraded.

Verified In practice the failover pool was a pool of one. 20 of 20 requests for the most-used free model were served by the same peer. Whether that reflects a thin market at that hour or a routing weakness, the operational consequence is identical: assume single-provider concentration until you have measured your own routes.

Claimed #11 #14 Deterrents against bad actors: a locally computed 0–100 trust score, an on-chain wash-trading registry that zeroes a proven offender's score to 0, per-seller price caps, allow and block lists, ghost-channel counts, and an external ERC-8004 identity registry.

Verified The wash-trading registry is real and observable. The independent analytics dashboard explicitly excludes wash-flagged sellers from its "organic" rankings and states that "wash-flagged sellers never appear here" — a credible anti-manipulation signal. Inference But wash-trading detection only catches collusive volume inflation. It does nothing about a seller quietly serving a cheaper model than its label.

5.6  Security and privacy

Genuinely well designed Verified #24 #25

  • Funds are held in a separate AntseedDeposits contract, distinct from the node's hot wallet.
  • The buyer's hot-wallet key is not the payment-signing key — documented, with an explicit risk-bound table.
  • EIP-191 signed discovery metadata with nonce replay guards and a 30-second clock-skew check.
  • X25519 + AES-256-GCM encrypted peer transport where supported, with fail-closed decoding.
  • Hard resource limits: 64 MiB frame cap, 64 inbound connections per IP, bounded buffers and stream durations.
  • The buyer recomputes cost from the seller's published prices before signing — a seller cannot unilaterally bill you.
  • GPL-3.0 open source, so all of the above is auditable.

Missing or worrying Verified

  • No independent audit of the contracts holding customer money. An independent reviewer confirmed none is published; BaseScan source-verification proves compilation, not safety. #53
  • No SOC 2, no ISO 27001, no GDPR processing agreement, no data-residency option. No evidence found
  • Sellers read your prompts. Only TEE providers should not — and TEE is a "future release" with roughly 2% adoption.
  • Payment metadata lands on a public chain. Amounts, timing and counterparties are permanent and publicly linkable.
  • Wallet addresses are pseudonymous, not anonymous. A stable address reused across requests is trivially correlatable.
  • No data-retention policy and no disclosure of what sellers log. No evidence found
  • CLI stores identity.key in plaintext by default on server and CLI installs unless you set ANTSEED_PASSPHRASE.
  • Gateway keys unencrypted by default — our gateway printed this warning at startup. Verified

5.8  Cost versus direct providers

Verified The fee structure is transparent and, in isolation, competitive. A flat 4% protocol fee is deducted from each settlement on Base mainnet — the contract source default is 2%, and the owner may set it as high as 10%. There is no platform subscription. First sessions with an unknown seller are hard-capped at $1, and if a seller disappears the buyer's funds are recoverable after a 15-minute grace period. Budgets can be enforced per key, per member and per workspace with hard 402 stops. #9 #26 #32

Verified But the network is far too small and too concentrated for prices to be meaningful. Lifetime gross settlement is about $317,000. Recent daily volume runs $228–$1,310. The seller side is highly concentrated: Gini coefficient 0.89, top three sellers take 38% of volume and the top ten take 66%. A market that small, with that few sellers, cannot be relied on to sustain a durable price advantage — a handful of sellers leaving would reprice the market overnight. #27 #28 #30

Verified Hidden costs observed: a 0.05 USDC relay fee on gasless wallet sweeps with a $1 minimum top-up; card payments routed through a third-party processor with regional limits and per-payment caps; and routing defaults that cap requests at $0.30 and reservations at $1.00 unless raised — which will silently break any workload with larger prompts. #16 #20

Inference There is also an unpriced risk cost that dominates the headline comparison: an open over-billing bug, unaudited contracts holding your funds, a seller reading your prompts, and no recourse when a model is quietly downgraded. Per token AntSeed may be cheaper; risk-adjusted, it is almost certainly not.

06

Red flags and credibility check

Ordered by how much damage each could do in production.

1 · An open bug that lets sellers over-charge buyers Verified

GitHub issue #1200, filed 14 October 2026 and still open at the time of this review: "Buyer over-signs SpendingAuth by minBudgetPerRequest ($0.50) on 402 catch-up." When a seller's delivered cost runs ahead of the buyer's last signature, the buyer signs a cumulative total $0.50 higher than requested. Because that signature is claimable on-chain, the seller can collect the extra. The issue reproduces live: a session worth $0.009 produced a signed authorisation of $0.50.

Why it matters: the seller's own source contains a comment warning against exactly this, so the team knows the invariant. It is a billing-correctness bug in a system where the buyer is the one paying. Do not send paid traffic until it is fixed. #48

2 · Contradictions between marketing and documentation Verified

Marketing saysThe documentation saysAssessment
"TEE-verified providers cannot see your prompts." #12"TEE provider verification is being rolled out in a future release." #8Direct contradiction. The privacy guarantee is advertised in the present tense while the docs place it in the future.
"700+ AI models." #31Our live buyer listed 561 model ids. AntSeedStats groups 282 models across 36 labs; 2,909 is the count of "advertised offerings". #28The 700+ figure is not reproducible under any definition we could construct. It appears to conflate listings with distinct models.
"Up to 97% below official API price." #54No benchmark, model-by-model table or methodology published anywhere.Unsourced marketing number, repeated verbatim by press outlets that did not independently verify it.
"Verification decides how much a provider's usage is worth." #12Verification affects reward eligibility, not buyer protection. #11An incentive mechanism presented as a consumer guarantee. Reasonable marketing framing, but it invites the wrong conclusion.

3 · Most of the supply is other companies' APIs Verified

Of the 1,826 listings our buyer exposed, 1,511 used the generic openai provider plugin. The independent seller rankings contain names that describe exactly what they are: Venice.ai Proxy, Mistral Relay Node, surplus-provider. AntSeed's own buyer dashboard flags most active sellers as "82%–98% buy elsewhere" — meaning the same buyers are obtaining the same models from the original vendors.

AntSeed's documentation states that "reselling subscription credentials is not allowed" and that providers must comply with upstream terms. We found no evidence that this is enforced, and no mechanism described that could enforce it.

Why it matters: if a meaningful share of listings are unauthorised resales of OpenAI or Anthropic subscriptions, those sellers can be terminated upstream without notice, taking your endpoint with them — and your company would be the party exposed to the upstream provider's terms-of-service breach. #27 #30

4 · Unaudited contracts holding customer funds Verified

Twenty contracts are deployed on Base mainnet, including AntseedDeposits (holds all buyer USDC) and AntseedChannels (payment-channel logic). An independent reviewer checked on-chain and reported no public smart-contract audit, noting that the payment-channel logic is custom code handling live USDC. BaseScan verification proves compilation, not safety. #5 #53

Why it matters: an exploit here is total and unrecoverable for every user simultaneously, and the incident-response capacity of a two-person team is limited. This is the classic reason not to place real money into unaudited, admin-controlled contracts.

5 · Team opacity Verified

No team page exists. No founder biographies, no jurisdiction of incorporation for the operating entity, no named officers on the website. The only named individual found anywhere is Amos Meiri, identified as CEO by Axios Pro. The GitHub history shows a two-person core (a 3,621-commit lead and a 248-commit second), and an independent review notes a "two-person team" with a "thin bench" for security response and treasury stewardship. The Foundation post says only: "We've been in this space since 2012."

Counterweight, stated fairly: Spark Capital led the round — a firm with a genuine track record — and the GitHub output is high quality, which is difficult to fake. But a serious investor vouching is not the same as knowing who is liable. #33 #53 #55

6 · The token Verified

The ANTS token page carries a permanent "TOKENS RESTRICTED" badge. Transfers are disabled at the contract level; an independent reviewer read transfersEnabled() as false directly on-chain and found no exchange quoting the asset. CoinMarketCap carries a listing with no market. AntSeed's own analytics dashboard warns that price sites listing an "ANTS" token index an unrelated impostor — a live phishing surface attributable to the naming.

Assessment: the fair-launch restriction is defensible in principle. The naming collision with an existing token is not, and it creates confusion in exactly the venues where confusion is most expensive. If you buy on AntSeed, do not touch ANTS. #12 #53 #60

7 · Minor but telling Verified

  • Broken link in their own navigation: "Live prices" points to /live-prices/, which returns 404. #35
  • Version confusion: npm ships 0.1.196; GitHub tags v0.2.65. An operator cannot tell which build they are running. #43 #50
  • Content-safety exposure: the default catalogue surfaced uncensored and roleplay models with categories like uncensored and roleplay. A corporate gateway that routes to the cheapest qualifying provider may route employees to a jailbroken model — and AntSeed's routing categories treat uncensored as a positive attribute. You need your own allowlist, not the default.
  • Low X engagement relative to reach: the launch post shows ~389,600 impressions against 423 likes (≈0.11%), with 254 bookmarks — high impressions, weak engagement. We could not verify the source of the impressions and are noting the ratio only. #61
  • Open issue: the Windows desktop app fails to start after an update, with a confusing error about decrypting the identity file. #51
  • Open issue: "Syria in the default restricted countries" — the geo-restriction list is itself under active dispute. #45
Counter-arguments to our own scepticism, stated plainly. Inference AntSeed is unusually candid. The light paper explicitly disclaims output accuracy, says TEE "proves the environment, not the model", tells buyers their prompts are visible to standard providers, publishes every contract address, ships a real troubleshooting guide, and publishes reputation weighting in full. Most projects would hide all of this. The documentation is the most credible artefact AntSeed produces. Our concern is not that they are hiding things — it is that what they have disclosed does not yet support a production deployment.

Risks if adopted anyway, with mitigations

RiskSeverityMitigationResidual risk after mitigation
Silent model downgradeHigh Run a model-fidelity suite — 50–100 known-answer prompts with expected outputs per model — against a rotating sample of sellers on a schedule. Pin providers explicitly (peerId@model) for anything critical, and alert on any change in the response model field. Medium. Pinning removes routing drift but locks you to that seller's quality, including its empty-response defect.
Over-billing via issue #1200High Do not send paid traffic until it is closed and released. Then reconcile every settlement line-by-line against your own token counts, and set a hard per-key daily budget so exposure is capped even if a similar bug recurs. Low once fixed and reconciled. Unacceptable before.
Prompt or PII exposure to sellersHigh Never send regulated or customer data. Scrub PII before the request. Restrict to an explicit seller allowlist. Do not rely on TEE until the verifier is live in a released version and you have confirmed it actually gates traffic. Medium. This is architectural. The only real fix is not sending sensitive data.
Provider outage with no failoverHigh Put a real provider (OpenAI, Anthropic or Together) in front of it as a fallback tier in your own router, so AntSeed never sits as the sole path to a customer-facing feature. Health-check the endpoint yourself, because nobody else will. Low — this is the single most important control.
Contract exploit or admin-key compromiseHigh Keep the deposited balance at the absolute minimum — fund per session, do not pre-load. Withdraw anything not in use. Keep the operator key separate. Do not treat AntSeed as a treasury. Medium. Caps the loss; cannot eliminate it.
Upstream seller termination (resale risk)Medium Prefer sellers running genuinely self-hosted models; check the provider plugin type in the catalogue — local-llm versus openai. Maintain more than one seller per model. Medium. Requires manual curation that will not scale.
Content-safety and jailbroken modelsMedium Configure an explicit model allowlist; do not rely on automatic routing, which ranks on price and trust only. Add output filtering downstream. Low once allowlisted.
Legal and compliance exposureHigh Obtain written legal sign-off on the Terms before any use. Note the Gibraltar arbitration and class-action waiver. Confirm your regulatory posture accepts that "the provider is an anonymous third party you cannot sue". Medium. May be an outright blocker.
Abandonment or key lossMedium Back up the secp256k1 identity key into your secrets manager. Maintain a standing alternative provider so a shutdown is an inconvenience, not an outage. Low.

The pragmatic alternative

Inference If the objective behind this evaluation is lower inference cost, there is a materially better path today: put OpenRouter in front of existing traffic for multi-model access and automatic fallback with a published status page, and negotiate volume pricing directly with Together or Fireworks for your heaviest models. Run your own spend-cap and fallback layer. You will likely pay more per token than AntSeed and far less than OpenAI list price, and you will keep an SLA, a DPA and a support escalation path — which AntSeed cannot currently offer at any price.
